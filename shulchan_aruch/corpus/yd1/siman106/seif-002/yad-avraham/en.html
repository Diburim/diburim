(Siman 106 seif 2) A piece that has milk in it that was cooked in a pot that has sixty to nullify the milk. Shach wrote: one should not challenge — it was certainly salted first, otherwise it would be forbidden on account of blood and would need to nullify all of it; and thus it was first forbidden through salting with the milk. One might say it deals with lean milk that forbids only kedei kelipah according to the Mechaber above siman 64 seif 9 — end of Shach's words. And behold proof that the source of this law is Rashba, and he deals explicitly with a piece that gid fat is attached to — see Beit Yosef end siman 107. And gid fat is lean milk, as explained siman 105 seifim 4, 5, and 9; and thus one must say what Shach wrote that it was forbidden in salting — his words are not precise. For in siman 105 he required removal for gid fat, and accordingly sixty was needed against the milk and also against the removal that was already forbidden in salting according to one who holds "a piece becomes nevelah" in other issurim; and as Tosafot Pesachim (daf 96) s.v. im yesh, etc., and Hagahot Alfasi from Mahariach there. Regarding whether it was forbidden in salting kedei kelipah. And it appears that even according to hagahah siman 91 seif 4 that wherever kelipah is required, if they did not peel and cooked thus b'di'eved it is permitted — this applies only where it was forbidden only kedei kelipah; but if it was first forbidden kedei netilah, all agree sixty is required against it if cooked thus without removal; and as is clearly implied in Tur, Shulchan Aruch, and Rama's hagahah beginning siman 96 regarding a radish cut with a meat knife that is forbidden kedei netilah — and if cooked in milk, sixty is required against it; and Rashba and Shulchan Aruch who did not mention estimating sixty against the removal — this is because they do not hold "a piece becomes nevelah" in other issurim, as Tosafot and Hagahot Alfasi from Mahariach wrote there in Pesachim — for R' Efrayim who does not hold chadichah na'aseh nevelah it is satisfactory — see there. Actually I say that if on account of this it does not apply, one may well say it deals when the piece was not salted beforehand. And what Shach wrote that otherwise sixty is needed to nullify all of it since it is forbidden on account of blood — there is to understand: for we hold above siman 98 that an olive's bulk of blood and an olive's bulk of milk that fell into a pot that has sixty olive-bulks of permitted — each joins to nullify the other's taste — see there; and accordingly here too — what difference that the piece was not salted? It is no worse than if it were all blood, for the milk joins to nullify it and it to the milk. And it deals where the meat was not salted in measure equal to the milk; and if there is sixty with the piece against the milk, there is no issur on account of milk, and also no issur on account of blood if there is sixty with the milk against the unsalted meat; and then the piece itself is permitted according to Shulchan Aruch siman 69 seif 11; and likewise for us, if they were not attached they would join to nullify one another; and the piece cooked in its blood would be forbidden if not for need, as hagahah wrote there in siman 69; and all I wrote in the Mechaber's words we were also forced to say in Rashi Yoreh Deah (daf 109), and I brought it siman 98 — see there:
